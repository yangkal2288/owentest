# Gates 1 and 2: Pain, positioning and script

The story is what makes people watch. Beautiful shots with no story read as a reel of effects. A film starts to work when it becomes one plain cause-and-effect chain that a stranger could retell.

## Gate 1: the pain bank

Research writes the script. Before any creative work, mine what customers actually said.

**Sources**, best first: sales and customer call transcripts, support conversations, churn and cancellation reasons, published case studies, reviews, community posts, competitor reviews (what people hate about the alternative).

**Build `PAIN-BANK.md`** (template in [../templates/PAIN-BANK.md](../templates/PAIN-BANK.md)):

- **Verbatim quotes**, tagged by permission: public (already published under a name), internal (anonymous use only), or unverified (the transcript may be wrong). Aim for 50 or more.
- **Filmable pain moments**: small, familiar objects doing one wrong thing. An unread badge climbing. A spreadsheet cell that says "chase?". A "send invoices" reminder snoozed for the fifth time. A progress bar stuck at 99%. Aim for 15 to 20.
- **Proof points**: real numbers from real customers or the product's own data.
- **Do-not-claim list**, carried over from Gate 0.

Then choose:

| Choice | What it is | Example (a fictional invoicing tool) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The pain, in one sentence** | The sharpest version, in the customer's words where possible | "The work is done, the invoice isn't sent, and the month ends without the money." |
| **One villain** | A single concrete object that causes the pain, so the viewer has something to blame | One draft invoice, sitting unsent in a folder |
| **2 or 3 felt numbers** | Specific, odd, tied to the villain on screen | "19 days late. $8,340 waiting. One unsent invoice." |
| **The longing** | What the viewer wants instead, as a scene | The payment notification arrives the same afternoon |
| **The USP** | The one thing only this product does, said plainly | It sends the invoice the moment the work is marked done |
| **The objection** | The viewer's reason not to switch, in their own words | "Moving all my clients over is a weekend I don't have", answered with how long it really takes |

**Research does not go on screen.** Tweet cards, Slack bubbles and testimonial quotes feel like an ad and slow the story down. The narrator says the pain; the picture shows the object.

**Don't show a busy dashboard.** Pain lives in small familiar objects, not in an app screen the viewer has to read.

### Lock

A one-page `BRIEF.md` with the pain sentence, villain, numbers, longing, USP, objection and the claims. No on-screen quotes.

## Gate 2: the script as narration

Write the whole film as spoken paragraphs before thinking about shots. If someone who only reads the script can retell the problem, the turn and the fix, the story works.

### The act template

These acts work for a product launch. Rename them for your story, but keep the order: effect before cause, pain before product, one mechanism before the platform.

| Act | Job | Length (of ~100 s) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hook** | A muted-friendly image of the effect in 1 to 3 seconds, plus a question | 3 to 6 s |
| **Pain** | Show the chain from cause to effect using small familiar objects | 20 to 30 s |
| **Culprit** | Name the villain and the cost (the felt numbers) | 6 to 10 s |
| **Turn** | A moment of calm or darkness, then the product arrives | 3 to 6 s |
| **Mechanism** | How the product fixes the villain, step by step, in one case | 12 to 18 s |
| **Platform** | What the product *is*: switching cost, what else you get, the category claim | 15 to 20 s |
| **Payoff** | The longing from Gate 1 comes true; the opening image rhymes | 4 to 8 s |
| **CTA** | Wordmark, one line, URL, a final gag on the last hit | 4 to 6 s |

The **platform** act is the one agents most often leave out. Viewers who like the magic moment immediately ask "can I switch, and what else do I get?" Put it in the first draft, with one visual idea per claim.

### Writing rules

- **Plain words.** Cryptic "cinematic" lines lose people. Write it the way you would explain it to a friend.
- **About 2.5 words per second** of finished film, leaving room for holds.
- **One cause-and-effect chain.** If a scene can be deleted without breaking the chain, delete it. A launch film is not a feature showcase.
- **Mark 2 or 3 crescendos**: the moments that get a setup, a hit, a sound and a hold. Typically the reveal of the villain, the product's arrival and the resolution.
- **Close the loop.** The last image rhymes with the first (the empty balance from the hook is full at the end), so the film rewatches well.
- **Write for the ear.** Sarcasm and irony are hard for TTS; mark the intended reading in the script ("ironic, not shouty") so Gate 3 can direct it.
- **Every claim is on the whitelist.**

Use [../templates/SCRIPT.md](../templates/SCRIPT.md). Each act is a paragraph, not a list of lines, because each act will be recorded as one take.

### Lock

The decision maker reads the script aloud (or hears a scratch TTS read of it) and approves the words.

## If there is no voice

A wordless film can work for a short teaser, but understanding is much harder without a narrator. If the decision maker prefers no voice, record a scratch voice anyway, show both, and let them choose. Without voice, on-screen type carries the script, so the kinetic type rules in [06-motion.md](06-motion.md) become the whole story.
